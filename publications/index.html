Who the Model Thinks You Are: Identity Disclosure, Epistemic Agency, and the Cost of Being Known by a Language Model

Arpan Verma, Aditya Upadhyay, Chandan Sah, Dev Utkarsh Pal, Harshit Gupta

When a user mentions a disability before an unrelated question, language models answer a picture of that user rather than the user, dropping formal structure, terminology and derivation, and the shift persists across turns. We argue that a model's implicit picture of its user is a design surface in its own right, and that accessibility should adapt format, not content.

Score Transport Cannot Recover Power: The Geometry of Correcting a Shifted Detector

Arpan Verma, Yash Tripathi

When a detector meets a new domain, remapping its scores can move the operating point but provably leaves the ROC curve unchanged; only feature-space maps that reorder examples recover discriminative power. We bound the cost of reusing a source-domain estimate of the nuisance subspace, and show why no source-only estimate suffices.

Lightweight Quantization Rotations with a Distortion Transfer Bound

Chandan Sah, Arpan Verma, Dev Utkarsh Pal, Aditya Upadhyay, Harshit Gupta

A two-stage randomized Hadamard transform can replace the dense random rotation used before scalar quantization: we prove its distortion gap to the Haar rotation is O(1/d), while the stored rotation shrinks from 8 MB to 0.25 KB at d = 1024. We measure the scaling, GPU latency and 2–4-bit grouped KV caches on Qwen2.5-0.5B and TinyLlama-1.1B.

When Higher-Order Geometry Fails to Help Phase Risk, Attention, and Rendering with JetRoPE

Arpan Verma, Chandan Sah, Dev Utkarsh Pal, Aditya Upadhyay, Harshit Gupta

JetRoPE, a parameter-free first-order extension of RayRoPE, is more geometrically faithful but depends on local derivatives that are harder to estimate. A phase-risk decomposition predicts where it should help, yet even with ground-truth geometry first order degrades rendering quality, showing where higher-order geometry breaks down.

Can an Injected-Failure Benchmark Pick Your Monitor? A Four-Part Validity Audit for Enterprise Agent Deployment

Dev Utkarsh Pal, Arpan Verma, Chandan Sah, Aditya Upadhyay, Harshit Gupta

Benchmarks built by injecting failures into agent traces get labels for free, but a correct label doesn't make an injected failure a stand-in for a real one. A four-part audit of 24,000 traces shows that a monitor selected on injected failures can lose accuracy once it meets natural failures in production.

From Linear Domain Invariance to Deployment Robustness: A Construct-Validity Audit of First-Moment Erasure

Yash Tripathi, Arpan Verma

Erasing domain information until a linear probe can no longer recover it does not by itself make a detector robust: across three benchmark families the criterion is fully met while unseen-domain false-positive rates show no detected improvement. We trace the gap to what first-moment summaries discard, and to a finite-sample artefact that cross-fitting removes.

Certified Rank-Stability: Risk-Controlling Leaderboard Claims Under Distribution Shift

Aditya Upadhyay, Arpan Verma, Chandan Sah, Dev Utkarsh Pal, Harshit Gupta

If model A beats model B on a clean benchmark, does the ranking survive realistic shifts? We compute exact rank-reversal risk over a declared grid of corruptions and use conformal risk control for sampled shifts; on COCO and Cityscapes only 12 of 30 clean pairwise claims qualify, all of them for detection.
